Your Mission

In this practice, you will build your first Arduino circuit: How to Blink an LED.

This practice has several challenges, and new ones will be added along the way. Every challenge starts in Tinkercad. After your simulation works and the teacher checks it, you will receive the materials needed to build that challenge using a real Arduino.

You must complete each challenge in order before moving on to the next one.

The Rule for Every Challenge

Simulate first. Build second.

  1. Watch or read the instructions for the current challenge.
  2. Build and test it in Tinkercad.
  3. Show the working simulation to the teacher.
  4. Receive the physical materials for that challenge.
  5. Build and test it in real life.
  6. Take pictures of the physical circuit and process with the classroom camera.
  7. Only then move to the next challenge.

IMPORTANT → You will not receive the physical components for a challenge until the Tinkercad version is complete, working, and checked by the teacher.

Challenge 1 · One LED Without a Breadboard

Start with the basic circuit: one external LED connected directly to the Arduino.

Tutorial 1: How to Blink an LED

  • 01Watch the tutorial.Pay attention to the LED polarity, resistor, Arduino pin, GND, and the Blink code.
  • 02Build the circuit in Tinkercad first.Use one Arduino, one LED, and one resistor.
  • 03Modify the example.Do not use pin 13. Choose another digital pin and change the delay value from the tutorial.
  • 04Run the simulation.Your LED must blink correctly before you continue.
  • 05Show your Tinkercad circuit to the teacher.After approval, you will receive the materials for the physical build.
  • 06Build the same circuit in real life.Use the same pin and delay values you already tested.
  • 07Document your work.The teacher will take pictures with the classroom camera. Keep your Tinkercad share link for the final presentation.

TEACHER CHECK → When Challenge 1 works in Tinkercad and in real life, show it to the teacher before you start Challenge 2.

Challenge 2 · One LED With a Breadboard

Now rebuild the blinking LED using a breadboard. The goal is to understand how the same circuit can be organized differently using breadboard rows and power connections.

Tutorial 2: How to Blink an LED with Breadboard

  • 01Watch the breadboard tutorial.Focus on how the Arduino, LED, resistor, and ground connection move onto the breadboard.
  • 02Build the breadboard version in Tinkercad first.Use one LED and reproduce the circuit using the breadboard.
  • 03Test the simulation.Make sure the LED blinks correctly and verify every connection before asking for materials.
  • 04Show your Tinkercad circuit to the teacher.Once it is approved, you will receive the breadboard and the physical components.
  • 05Build the breadboard circuit in real life.Compare your physical connections with your working Tinkercad model.
  • 06Document your work.The teacher will take pictures. Save the Tinkercad share link for the presentation.

TEACHER CHECK → When the breadboard version works in Tinkercad and in real life, show it to the teacher before you start Challenge 3.

Challenge 3 · Add a Second LED

There is no tutorial for this challenge. Use what you learned in Challenges 1 and 2 to figure out how to add a second LED to your breadboard circuit.

YOUR CHALLENGE → How can you add another LED, give it the connections it needs, and control it from the Arduino without removing the first LED?

  • 01Plan before connecting.Think about what the second LED needs: a digital pin, resistor, correct polarity, and a path to ground.
  • 02Add the second LED in Tinkercad first.Use the breadboard and the buses/rails where appropriate.
  • 03Update the code.Choose how the second LED will behave and make sure the Arduino code matches your new connections.
  • 04Test and troubleshoot.Do not ask for the second LED until the Tinkercad version works.
  • 05Show the teacher.Explain what you changed and why you think it works.
  • 06Build the final circuit in real life.Add the second LED to the working breadboard circuit.
  • 07Document the final circuit.The teacher will take pictures of the two-LED circuit. Save the final Tinkercad share link.

Challenge 4 · Blinking LED + Push Button

Now add a push button to your LED circuit. Use what you learned in Challenges 1 to 3 and the tutorial below to make the Arduino read the button.

Tutorial 3: Pushbutton Digital Input With Arduino in Tinkercad

  • 01Watch the tutorial.Pay attention to how the push button connects to the Arduino and to the code that reads it (pinMode, digitalRead, and if).
  • 02Build the circuit in Tinkercad first.Use one Arduino, one LED, one resistor, and one push button on the breadboard.
  • 03Write your code.Make the LED blink when the button is pressed. Choose your own pins and delay values, do not copy the tutorial exactly.
  • 04Run the simulation.Your LED must react to the button correctly before you continue.
  • 05Show your Tinkercad circuit to the teacher.After approval, you will receive the materials for the physical build.
  • 06Build the same circuit in real life.Use the same pins and delay values you already tested.
  • 07Document your work.The teacher will take pictures with the classroom camera. Keep your Tinkercad share link for the final presentation.

Create your final presentation, in the same order as the practice.
