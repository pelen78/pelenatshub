Your Mission

Before you build anything, you need to know what you are building with. This project is your first look at the Arduino — the small board that will run almost everything you make in this class.

You will research it and put everything on one page: an infographic in Genially. Not a slideshow. One page, top to bottom, that someone can read in a minute.

Why one page? Slides let you hide weak answers behind a lot of empty space. A single page does not. You have 150 words for the whole thing, so every word has to earn its place. That is the hard part of this assignment, and it is on purpose.

Then you install the Arduino IDE on your laptop. Next class we stop reading and start building — but only if the software is already running.

Seven Blocks

Your page needs these seven blocks, in this order. Short phrases only — no paragraphs.

  • 01What is an Arduino?Your own words. Under 40 words.
  • 02Why learn it?One reason, connected to something you actually want to build.
  • 03What is a sketch?Show three common commands and what each one does.
  • 04Pros and cons of the Arduino programming languageThree of each.
  • 05Four projects you want to buildA photo and one line for each one.
  • 06What is new in Arduino?Uno R4, Uno Q, and the Qualcomm news.
  • 07Proof of installA screenshot of the Arduino IDE open on your laptop.

Block 03 · this is what a command looks like

pinMode(13, OUTPUT);set a pin to input or output

digitalWrite(13, HIGH);turn a pin on or off

delay(1000);wait one second

Those three are an example of the format, not your answer. Find your own three and explain them in your own words.

Setting up Genially

Do these four steps. We'll work on the content together in class.

  1. Create your free account. Sign up with your school email and choose the free plan — never enter payment information.
    Open genially.com
  2. Click Create and choose Infographic. Pick a vertical template. Do not start from a blank page for this one — the template keeps your spacing consistent.

  4. Click Publish and copy your link. That link is what you turn in — see Turning it in below.

Check it on your phone before you publish. Open your own link on your phone and read it top to bottom. If text overlaps or you have to zoom in to read it, fix it.

Installing the Arduino IDE

The IDE is the program where you write code and send it to the board. Install it before next class — we won't wait for downloads.

  1. Download Arduino IDE 2.3.10 for your operating system. Get version 2 — version 1.8 is old and no longer updated.
    Open the Arduino download page
  2. Install it. On macOS, the first time you open it you will get a security warning. That is normal. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and allow it.
  3. Open the IDE. Go to File → New Sketch and save it as firstname_blink.
  4. Take a screenshot of the IDE with your sketch open. That screenshot is block 07 of your page.
  5. Bring your laptop to the next class with the IDE already installed.

Can't install it? If your laptop blocks the installation or you are on a Chromebook, tell me before the due date — there is an online version we can use instead. Telling me the day it's due is too late.

Turning it in

You turn in a link, not a file. Nothing gets emailed, nothing gets downloaded.

  1. In Genially, click Publish. Your page is not visible to anyone until you do this.
  2. Copy your link. It looks like view.genially.com/…
  3. Open Google Classroom and find this assignment, Arduino First Blink.
  4. Click Add or create → Link, paste your Genially link, and click Add.
  5. Click Turn in. If you don't press Turn in, it isn't submitted.

Test your own link first. Open it in a private window, or send it to yourself. If it asks for a password or says the page is private, go back to Genially and publish it properly. A link I can't open counts as not turned in.

Two-Minute Presentation

Everyone presents their page to the class. Two minutes each. You will be asked:

→ Explain block 01 without reading it off the screen.

→ Which of your four projects would you build first, and why?

→ What did you look up yourself, and what did you ask an AI?

The point: if you can't explain it out loud, it isn't yours yet. That's not a punishment — it's how you find out what you actually understood.
