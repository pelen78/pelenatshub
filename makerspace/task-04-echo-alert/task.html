Your Mission

Bats find their way in the dark with echoes. The HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor does the same thing.

It sends a sound that humans cannot hear. Then it measures how long the echo takes to come back. The Arduino turns that time into centimeters.

This practice has research and challenges. Research comes first. Every challenge starts in Tinkercad.

You turn in two things: a Google Doc with your research answers, and a presentation with your circuit.

The Rule for Every Challenge

Research first. Simulate second. Build third.

  1. Answer the research questions in a Google Doc.
  2. Build and test the challenge in Tinkercad.
  3. Show the working simulation to the teacher.
  4. Receive the physical materials for that challenge.
  5. Build and test it in real life.
  6. Take pictures of the physical circuit and process with the classroom camera.

IMPORTANT → Do not open Tinkercad until your research answers are done. You will not receive the sensor until your Tinkercad version works.

Challenge 1 · The Echo Alert Circuit

The LED stays off. When an object gets 5 to 7 cm close to the sensor, the LED turns on. When the object moves away, the LED turns off again.

Parts you need

  • Arduino Uno R3
  • Small breadboard
  • Ultrasonic Distance Sensor (4 pins)
  • 1 LED, any color
  • 1 resistor, 220 Ω
  • Jumper wires

Wiring

  • Sensor VCC to 5V
  • Sensor GND to GND
  • Sensor TRIG to pin 9
  • Sensor ECHO to pin 10
  • LED long leg (+) to pin 7 through the resistor
  • LED short leg (−) to GND
  • 01Start a new design in Tinkercad Circuits.Name it EchoAlert_YourName.
  • 02Add the sensor.Search "Ultrasonic Distance Sensor". Pick the one with 4 pins, not 3.
  • 03Place the parts and wire them.Follow the wiring card above. Red wires for power, black wires for ground.
  • 04Add the starter code.Open Code, change Blocks to Text, and paste the code below.
  • 05Test the distance.Start the simulation and open the Serial Monitor. Move the object. The number must change.
  • 06Write your if and else.Turn the LED on when the object is close. Pick a number from 5 to 7 cm.
  • 07Show your Tinkercad circuit to the teacher.After approval, you will receive the materials for the physical build.
  • 08Build the same circuit in real life.Use the same pins and the same distance you already tested.
  • 09Document your work.The teacher will take pictures with the classroom camera. Keep your Tinkercad share link for the presentation.

Starter code

// Echo Alert - Your Full Name Here

int trigPin = 9;
int echoPin = 10;
int ledPin  = 7;

long duration;
int distance;

void setup() {
  pinMode(trigPin, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(echoPin, INPUT);
  pinMode(ledPin, OUTPUT);
  Serial.begin(9600);
}

void loop() {
  // 1. Send a 10 microsecond pulse from the trigger
  digitalWrite(trigPin, LOW);
  delayMicroseconds(2);
  digitalWrite(trigPin, HIGH);
  delayMicroseconds(10);
  digitalWrite(trigPin, LOW);

  // 2. Measure how long the echo takes to come back
  duration = pulseIn(echoPin, HIGH);

  // 3. Turn time into centimeters
  distance = duration * 0.034 / 2;

  Serial.print("Distance: ");
  Serial.print(distance);
  Serial.println(" cm");

  // 4. YOUR TURN Turn the LED on when the object is close,
  //    and off when it is far. Use if and else.

  delay(100);
}

It does not work? Check this first

I cannot change the distance in the simulation.
Start the simulation first. Then click the sensor. A circle appears in front of it. Drag the circle closer and farther.
The Serial Monitor always says 0 cm.
Check that TRIG and ECHO are not swapped. You write to TRIG. You read from ECHO.
The LED is always on.
Check your comparison. distance <= 7 means close. Did you write an else to turn it off?
The LED never turns on.
The LED may be backwards. The long leg (+) goes to the pin side.
