Stage 1
Desired Results
What do we want students to understand, know, and be able to do?
Established Goals / Standards
ISTE 4 — Innovative Designer Students use design processes to solve problems and create innovative solutions.
ISTE 5 — Computational Thinker Students break down problems and use technology to explore solutions.
Maker Mindset Curiosity, iteration, and hands-on problem solving as a mode of learning.
SEP — Science & Engineering Practices Asking questions, defining problems, and designing solutions.
Understandings & Questions
Enduring Understandings
  • Making is a form of thinking. Building something reveals how you understand a problem.
  • The best makers know their tools and choose the right one for each challenge.
  • Curiosity and exploration are the first steps in every creative process.
Essential Questions
  • What does it mean to be a maker?
  • What can I build with the tools and technology available to me?
  • How does exploring what others have made help me discover what I want to create?
Knowledge & Skills
Students will Know…
  • What tools and equipment are available in the MakerSpace (3D printer, Arduino, Fusion 360, Blender, Java)
  • What each unit of the course covers and what they will be expected to build
  • How to use a Google Doc to document ideas, research, and inspiration
Students will be able to Do…
  • Identify and describe at least 5 real-world maker projects that interest them
  • Articulate a dream project and connect it to a specific unit or tool
  • Create a Maker Profile that communicates their identity and goals as a maker
Stage 2
Assessment Evidence
How will we know if students have achieved the desired results?
Performance Task
Students create a Maker Profile using any digital tool of their choice. The profile must include their name, 3 words that describe them as a maker, their dream project, the unit they're most excited about, at least one image, and their Inspiration Doc A Google Doc with at least 5 maker projects they found during the week, noting what each is and why it caught their attention, with their favorite clearly marked. Students share their profile in a gallery on Thursday and vote for the most creative dream project.
Grading Criteria
Rubric Description
All 6 Required Elements Name, 3 maker words, dream project, favorite unit, at least one image, and Inspiration Doc.
Inspiration Doc At least 5 projects documented with a brief description of each and a clearly marked favorite with explanation
Effort & Creativity Profile feels personal and intentional.
Participation Actively engaged during tool tour, demos, and exploration days
Thursday Reflection "What surprised you most about this space, and what are you most excited to learn this year?" Submitted in Google Classroom
Teacher Observation Notes (Diagnostic)
While students explore on Tuesday and Wednesday, observe and note: Who gravitates toward which tools naturally? Who is intimidated vs. who dives in immediately? Who asks thoughtful questions about the machines? Who's already had experience with Arduino, 3D printing, or coding? This informal data helps inform grouping, pacing, and project scaffolding for later units.
Stage 3
Learning Plan
What sequence of teaching and learning experiences will get students there?
Mon Inspiration & Intro
Activities
  • Watch inspiring maker projects as a class
  • Students record at least 5 projects in their Inspiration Doc
  • Mark favorite and explain why in the doc
  • Discuss: if you could build anything, what would it be?
  • Assignment walkthrough
Teacher Role
  • Present video examples
  • Facilitate class discussion
  • Set up Google Doc template in Classroom
  • Clarify assignment expectations
Resources
  • Curated maker project videos
  • Google Classroom: Inspiration Doc template
  • Assignment instruction page
  • Projector / screen
Tue Tool Tour
Activities
  • Tour the tools around the classroom
  • Demos: 3D printer, Arduino, design software
  • Students add new discoveries to their Inspiration Doc
Teacher Role
  • Lead tool demos at each station
  • Encourage questions and curiosity
  • Begin informal observation of student reactions
Resources
  • 3D printer, Arduino kits
  • Fusion 360, Blender installed on computers
  • Student Inspiration Docs
Wed Build Profile
Activities
  • Build their Maker Profile using any digital tool
  • Include all 6 required elements
  • Submit Inspiration Doc and profile to Google Classroom
Teacher Role
  • Circulate and observe tool choices
  • Take diagnostic notes on skill level and interests
  • Remind students of requirements.
Resources
  • Classroom computers
  • Google Slides, Canva, or tool of choice
  • Assignment instruction page on screen
Thu Gallery & Reflection
Activities
  • Gallery share: each student shows their Maker Profile
  • Class votes for most creative dream project
  • Written reflection in Google Classroom
  • Prompt: "What surprised you most about this space, and what are you most excited to learn this year?"
Teacher Role
  • Facilitate gallery and voting
  • Debrief the week as a class
  • Share observations across the group
  • Preview what's coming in Unit 1
Resources
  • Projector / TV screen
  • Google Classroom reflection assignment
  • Voting activity (Mentimeter or show of hands)
