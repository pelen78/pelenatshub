Your Mission

In Task 1, you researched what Arduino is, how it is used, and the tools programmers use to work with it. Now you go one step further: you become the teacher.

You will be assigned one Arduino topic. Research it and prepare a short presentation to teach the class. Your goal is to help everyone understand one important Arduino concept before we start working with the board.

Your Process

Three steps, in order

  1. Research your topic and write it up in a Google Doc.
  2. Convert that Google Doc to a PDF.
  3. Build your presentation from that PDF: AI is allowed here, only to help build the slides.
Arduino Topics

Each student will be assigned one of the topics below.

  • 01What Is Arduino?Explain what Arduino is, what it can control, and give a few examples of things people build with it.
  • 02Arduino Hardware OverviewIntroduce the main parts of an Arduino board: microcontroller, digital pins, analog pins, power pins, USB connection, reset button, and other important parts.
  • 03Install and Set Up the Arduino IDEShow how to download and install the Arduino IDE. Explain how to connect an Arduino board, select the correct board, and select the correct port.
  • 04Arduino IDE and Sketch OverviewExplain the main parts of the Arduino IDE and what an Arduino sketch is. Introduce setup() and loop() and explain how a sketch is uploaded to the board.
  • 05Arduino Syntax BasicsExplain basic rules used when writing Arduino code: braces { }, semicolons ;, comments, functions, and why syntax must be written correctly.
  • 06Understanding VariablesExplain what a variable is, why programmers use variables, and show a few simple Arduino examples using variables such as int and bool.
  • 07Understanding Inputs and OutputsExplain what an input and an output are in Arduino. Give examples of inputs such as buttons or sensors and outputs such as LEDs, buzzers, or motors. Explain how Arduino can receive information and then control something.
  • 08How to Blink an LEDShow how the classic Blink program works. Explain what the code does and how Arduino turns an LED on and off. Connect the example to concepts taught in the earlier mini lessons.
Step 1 · Research It in a Google Doc

This assignment is designed to practice real research and reading. AI tools may not be used to find, summarize, or write the information in your Google Doc.

Everything you plan to teach starts here, written by you, in your own words.

  1. Create a new Google Doc.
  2. At the top, add a Title, your Name, and the Date.
  3. Research your assigned topic using at least two reliable sources. Official Arduino pages, documentation, educational websites, and teacher-provided links are good choices. Open and read them yourself, do not use AI-generated search summaries as a source.
  4. Write what you learned in your own words.
  5. At the end of the Doc, add a Sources list: the title or website name and the URL for each source, at least two.
  6. When it's ready, go to File → Download → PDF Document and convert your Google Doc to a PDF.

Keep that PDF. You will use it to build your presentation in Step 2, and you will submit it along with your finished slides.

Step 2 · Build Your Presentation

Turn the research from your PDF into 3–5 clear slides, about 5 minutes long.

  • 01Explain the topic in your own words.
  • 02Teach the most important vocabulary or concepts.
  • 03Include at least one visual, example, or demonstration.
  • 04Explain why the topic is importantwhen using Arduino.
  • 05End with one quick questionto check the class's understanding.

Don't read your slides word for word. Use them to support what you are teaching, not to replace it.

AI is allowed here, for one job only

  • AI may not do your research or write your explanations. That already has to be sitting in your Step 1 PDF, written by you.
  • AI may not generate the presentation's content or information for you.
  • AI may help you build, design, or lay out the slides, using only the information already in your PDF.
  • You decide what goes on each slide. AI is a design tool here, not a source.

READ THIS AGAIN → AI can help you BUILD the presentation. It cannot RESEARCH or WRITE the content, that has to already be in your PDF.

Turn In

You submit two files once your mini lesson is ready.

  1. Your research PDF from Step 1, with your Title, Name, Date, and Sources.
  2. Your finished presentation from Step 2.

Both files, every time. Turning in only the presentation, without the research PDF behind it, counts as incomplete.

Reminder

The goal is not to give a perfect or advanced lecture. Your job is to make one Arduino idea clear, simple, and useful for the rest of the class.
